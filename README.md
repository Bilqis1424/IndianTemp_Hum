# IndianTemp_Hum – Stacked Ensemble Forecasting for Temperature and Humidity

**Multi-Location Temporal Forecasting of Near-Surface Temperature and Relative Humidity Across Diverse Climatic Regions of India**

This repository contains the complete, reviewer-responsive pipeline used to produce the results reported in `Methodology_Report.docx`. It resolves all eleven major review comments from the three review rounds; see Section 7 of the report for the full comment-by-comment crosswalk.

The pipeline file `indianweather files/indian_weather_pipeline_v3_arima_fixed.py` is the current version and includes the two corrections made in response to the most recent review round — the graphical-abstract inconsistency and the ARIMA baseline anomaly. A summary of what changed relative to the earlier `indian_weather_pipeline_v3_final.py` is provided in the [Changelog](#what-changed-relative-to-indian_weather_pipeline_v3_finalpy) section below.

What Changed Relative to indian_weather_pipeline_v3_final.py
The ARIMA-corrected pipeline was produced in direct response to the two outstanding points in the most recent review round. Two substantive changes were made; both are documented in more detail in Section 7 of the report.
Change 1 — Fair ARIMA evaluation
(Responds to “ARIMA needs investigation/clarification”)
The previous ARIMA result (Temp R² = 0.069, Hum R² = −0.061) was an evaluation artefact, not a property of ARIMA on this data. Two problems existed in the old pipeline:

ARIMA was fitted and evaluated on the raw per-city series with its own train/test split, which did not coincide with the split used by persistence, LSTM, and the stacked ensemble.
The aggregate ARIMA metric was reported as the mean of per-city R² values, whereas persistence and the stacked ensemble were reported as pooled R² over the full held-out set. Averaging R² across 543 cities — many of which have short, low-variance test windows — biases the aggregate sharply downward even when the underlying predictions are of comparable quality.

ARIMA is now evaluated on exactly the same held-out rows as every other model (the train/test boundary is defined by intersecting each city’s series with the same test index used downstream), and its metrics are reported pooled over those rows, in original physical units (°C and %RH). Row-level predictions with city, datetime, y_true, and y_pred are saved so the alignment can be verified independently.
After the correction:

Temperature R² rises from 0.069 → 0.926
Humidity R² rises from −0.061 → 0.797

These values are comfortably in line with the persistence and stacked-ensemble baselines; ARIMA is the best model on humidity R².
Change 2 — Fair persistence baseline
The previous persistence baseline used a single global shift(1) on the concatenated multi-city test set, which for the first row of each city predicted from the last row of a different city. That cross-city contamination made persistence look artificially worse than it should.
In the corrected pipeline, persistence is computed per-city (ŷ_t = y_{t-1} within the same city). The corrected persistence MAE is therefore stronger — 0.783 °C instead of 1.009 °C — which is why the stacked ensemble no longer shows a large MAE advantage over persistence. This is an honest correction; the manuscript now states plainly that the stacked ensemble matches but does not beat per-city persistence on temperature MAE, and that its advantage over ARIMA is confined to a small RMSE reduction on temperature.
Additional improvements
Three further improvements were also made to the feature set and modelling pipeline:

(a) Extended temporal features (lags 1–8, rolling means/stds over 24 h and 48 h, 24-hour change, lagged atmospheric covariates)
(b) Per-city additive bias correction, fitted on a chronological validation slice and shrunk toward zero
(c) Three clearly-labelled R² definitions reported side by side (pooled, per-location with bootstrap CIs, and R²_anom — a within-city skill score against a per-city climatological mean)

The three-way R² distinction is what prevents a recurrence of the graphical-abstract mismatch flagged in the previous review round.
